\begin{abstract}
We present \GB{}, a method that predicts the consensus secondary structure of an RNA alignment, pseudoknots included. It combines the Knudsen--Hein (KH-99) stochastic context-free grammar with a phylogenetic model of unpaired and paired alignment columns, and parses the alignment into two nested layers: a first CYK pass predicts the nested structure, and a second pass predicts pseudoknotted pairs among the remaining columns at a tuned cost per pair. The substitution model and grammar are estimated from 337 Rfam families, and the decoding settings are tuned on 100 further families. The tuning method was selected by cross-validation grouped by Rfam clan. On a clan-disjoint test set of 246 Rfam alignments with literature-derived structures, 47 of them pseudoknotted, \GB{} reaches a base-pair F1 of 0.759 (95\% CI 0.723--0.792). That is statistically indistinguishable from RNAalifold (0.767) and IPknot (0.757). On pseudoknotted base pairs its F1 is 0.541, against 0.113 for IPknot. It detects a pseudoknot in 60\% of pseudoknotted alignments at a specificity of 94\%, and predicts an alignment in a median of 15\,ms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
RNA function depends on base pairing, which forms helical stems and loops. When several homologous sequences are aligned, compensatory mutations reveal which columns pair, so the consensus structure of an alignment can be inferred more reliably than the structure of one sequence. Pseudoknots, in which pairs cross, occur in many functional RNAs, but most consensus predictors either exclude them or predict them poorly.

KH-99 \cite{knudsen1999} and Pfold \cite{knudsen2003} combine a stochastic context-free grammar (SCFG) with a phylogenetic model of the alignment. SCFGs describe nested structures only. \GB{} extends this approach to pseudoknots with a second parsing layer and asks whether such an extension is competitive. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A two-layer parser} with helix initiation and extension ratios, a pseudoknot pair ratio and an evidence weight (Section~\ref{sec:model});
    \item \textbf{A benchmark} of 683 Rfam alignments with literature-derived structures, split by clan into training, tuning and test sets, scored with pseudoknot-specific metrics against RNAalifold and IPknot (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark});
    \item \textbf{A tuning protocol} that chooses between tuning methods by clan-grouped cross-validation (Section~\ref{sec:tuning});
    \item \textbf{An evaluation} that separates overall accuracy, pseudoknot accuracy, ablations and running time, with bootstrap confidence intervals (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Consensus structure prediction.} RNAalifold \cite{bernhart2008rnaalifold} from the ViennaRNA Package \cite{lorenz2011viennarna} folds an alignment by combining averaged free energies with a covariation term; it predicts nested structures. KH-99 and Pfold use an SCFG with phylogenetic column likelihoods, and PPfold \cite{sukosd2012ppfold} parallelises Pfold.

\textbf{Pseudoknots.} Exact dynamic programming for general pseudoknots, such as the algorithm of Rivas and Eddy \cite{rivas1999pseudoknots}, runs in $O(n^6)$ time; stochastic multiple context-free grammars \cite{kato2006stochastic} and pair stochastic tree adjoining grammars \cite{matsui2005pair} model restricted classes at lower but still super-cubic cost. IPknot \cite{sato2011ipknot} decomposes a structure into nested levels and selects pairs from base-pairing probabilities by integer programming; it accepts alignments, which makes it the closest pseudoknot-capable baseline for our setting. Knotify \cite{andrikos2023knotify} detects pseudoknots with syntactic pattern recognition, and deep-learning models such as E2Efold \cite{chen2022rna} and SPOT-RNA \cite{singh2019rna} predict pairing matrices from single sequences.

\GB{} stays within cubic-time CYK. Like IPknot it builds pseudoknotted structures from nested layers, but it scores each layer with a phylogenetic SCFG rather than with thermodynamic pair probabilities.

\subsection{Structure grammar}
The KH-99 grammar in Chomsky normal form is
\begin{align*}
    &S \rightarrow L\ S \mid \$M\ E \mid s \qquad L \rightarrow \$M\ E \mid s\\
    &F \rightarrow \$M\ E \mid L\ S \qquad\ \, \$M \rightarrow B\ F\\
    &B \rightarrow d \qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \, E \rightarrow d
\end{align*}
$S$ generates a loop region of one or more elements, and $L$ one element: an unpaired base $s$ or a helix. $\$M\,E$ generates a base pair $d\,F\,d$, whose interior $F$ is either another pair (stacking) or a loop of at least two elements. Every nested structure has exactly one derivation, so the maximum-likelihood rule probabilities are the relative rule frequencies in the training structures.

\subsection{Phylogenetic column model}
Unpaired columns evolve under a $4\times 4$ rate matrix $R$, and paired columns under a $16\times 16$ rate matrix over dinucleotides \cite{knudsen1999}. For every tree, Felsenstein's pruning algorithm \cite{felsenstein1981} gives $\log P_{\text{single}}(i)$ for each column and $\log P_{\text{pair}}(i,j)$ for each pair of columns, with gaps as unknown nucleotides \cite{knudsen2003}. Identical columns have identical likelihoods, so both are computed once per distinct column pattern.

\textbf{Estimation.} Each training family has the same total weight, split over its sequences. For ordered pairs of sequences with at least 85\% identity over columns where both have a base, substitutions are counted in unpaired and paired columns (the latter symmetrised) and divided by the expected exposure,
$$R_{XY} = \frac{c_{XY}}{P_s\,P_X \sum_p t_p N_p},\qquad R_{XX} = -\sum_{Y\neq X} R_{XY},$$
where $t_p$ is the tree distance and $N_p$ the number of comparable columns of sequence pair $p$, $P_X$ the frequency of $X$ and $P_s$ the fraction of unpaired positions; the paired matrix uses $2/P_p$ in place of $1/P_s$.

\textbf{Trees.} Trees have a neighbour-joining topology \cite{saitou1987} on Jukes--Cantor distances, with maximum-likelihood branch lengths under $R$ (golden-section search per branch with inside and outside partial likelihoods), rooted at the midpoint. For estimation this is done in two stages: neighbour-joining branch lengths give a first model, whose rates then set the branch lengths for the final estimate.

\subsection{Two-layer parsing}
\label{sec:parser}
Extending the grammar with the column evidence gives the Viterbi recursions below; all terms are log-probabilities and $q$ denotes rule probabilities.
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Pair}(i,j) &= M(i,j{-}1) + \log P_{\text{pair}}(i,j) + \chi(i,j)\log\rcross\\
M(i,j) &= \log q_{\$M\to BF} + \max\big(F_{\text{st}}(i{+}1,j) + \log\rext,\\
&\qquad\qquad F_{\text{lp}}(i{+}1,j) + \log\rinit\big)\\
F_{\text{st}}(i,j) &= \log q_{F\to \$M E} + \mathrm{Pair}(i,j)\\
F_{\text{lp}}(i,j) &= \log q_{F\to LS} + \max_k L(i,k) + S(k{+}1,j)\\
L(i,j) &= \log q_{L\to \$M E} + \mathrm{Pair}(i,j) \quad (i<j)\\
S(i,j) &= \max\big(\log q_{S\to \$M E} + \mathrm{Pair}(i,j),\\
&\qquad \log q_{S\to LS} + \max_k L(i,k) + S(k{+}1,j)\big)
\end{align*}
For a single column, $S(i,i) = \log q_{S\to s} + \log P_{\text{single}}(i)$, and likewise for $L$. The helix initiation ratio $\rinit$ applies to a pair that closes a loop, i.e.\ the innermost pair of a helix, and the helix extension ratio $\rext$ to a pair stacked on another pair; with $\rinit<\rext$, helices are cheaper to extend than to start. Splitting $F$ into its stacked and loop derivations applies the ratios exactly.

\textbf{First layer.} The parser runs on all columns and returns a nested structure.

\textbf{Second layer.} The parser runs again on the columns that the first layer leaves unpaired and returns a second nested set of pairs, which may cross the first. Each of its pairs is weighted by the pseudoknot pair ratio $\rpk$, and by $\rcross$ once for every first-layer pair it crosses ($\chi$ counts these). With $\rpk<1$ a pseudoknot helix must be better supported by the alignment than a nested one.

The two layers describe bi-secondary structures, the class IPknot predicts with two levels. The search is greedy: the second layer can only use columns the first leaves free. We also evaluated a \emph{joint refinement} that re-parses each layer given the other and keeps a round only if the joint score of both layers increases (Section~\ref{sec:tuning}).

\subsection{Evidence weight and hairpin loops}
\label{sec:weight}
Every column log-likelihood is multiplied by the evidence weight $\beta$ before parsing. The phylogenetic model treats columns as independent and sequences as related only through the tree, which overstates the evidence of a small alignment; with $\beta<1$ the grammar counts for more. A pair $(i,j)$ is admitted only if $j-i>\hmin$ in alignment coordinates, with $\hmin=3$; the bare grammar allows loops of two columns.

\textbf{Output.} The predicted pairs are drawn with the largest nested subset as \texttt{()}, the largest nested subset of the rest as \texttt{[]}, and so on (exact interval dynamic programming). This affects only the drawing, not the pairs or any score.

\textbf{Implementation and verification.} Each span length of the chart is filled in one vectorised step, and the bifurcation reads contiguous array slices, so a pass costs $O(n^3)$ arithmetic with $O(n)$ interpreter iterations. The CYK recursions (for $\hmin=2$ and $3$), the structure scoring used by refinement, the largest-nested-subset program and pattern-compressed likelihoods are all tested against exhaustive enumeration or direct computation.

\section{Benchmark and Protocol}
\label{sec:benchmark}
\subsection{Data}
The benchmark is built from Rfam 15.1 \cite{kalvari2021rfam} seed alignments. Only the 769 families whose consensus structure is annotated as published or taken from PseudoBase \cite{vanbatenburg2000pseudobase} are used. Structures annotated as predicted (by RNAalifold, CMfinder and similar tools) are excluded, because they would reward agreement with the tool that made them. Families of one Rfam clan are kept together, and clans are shuffled with a fixed seed within two strata (with or without a pseudoknotted family) and dealt 50/15/35\% to training, tuning and test.

Training families keep up to 100 seed sequences. Each tuning and test family contributes one alignment of 4--12 random seed sequences, the setting in which a user supplies a small alignment. In every alignment, columns where at most half of the sequences have a base are removed together with the reference pairs that use them; alignments with fewer than three reference pairs, and tuning or test alignments longer than 400 columns, are skipped (Table~\ref{tab:data}).

\subsection{Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{All base pairs:} positive predictive value (PPV), sensitivity and F1, pooled over the alignments of a set.
    \item \textbf{Pseudoknotted pairs:} the same measures restricted to pairs that cross at least one other pair of their own structure. This definition covers both helices of a pseudoknot and does not depend on bracket layers.
    \item \textbf{Pseudoknot detection:} per alignment, sensitivity (a pseudoknot is predicted when the reference has one) and specificity (none is predicted when the reference has none).
\end{itemize}
Confidence intervals come from 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples of alignments; differences between methods use paired resamples.

\subsection{Baselines}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RNAalifold} 2.7.2, consensus minimum free energy, default parameters (nested structures only).
    \item \textbf{IPknot} 1.1.0 in aligned mode, mixing averaged McCaskill and RNAalifold base-pairing probabilities as recommended for alignments, with default thresholds (pseudoknots allowed).
    \item \textbf{KH-99 CYK}: our parser with one layer, all ratios 1, $\beta=1$ and $\hmin=2$.
\end{itemize}
Knotify, SPOT-RNA and E2Efold predict from single sequences and would need a rule for combining per-sequence predictions into a consensus. They were not evaluated.

\section{Tuning}
\label{sec:tuning}
\subsection{Tuning methods}
Seven settings are tunable: $\rinit$ and $\rext$ of each layer, $\rcross$, $\rpk$ and $\beta$. Ratios take values $2^{k/3}$ for $k=-8,\dots,8$ (0.16--6.35), $\rpk\in\{10^{-k/4}: k=0,\dots,8\}$ (1--0.01) and $\beta\in\{0.4,0.5,\dots,1.1,1.25\}$; $\hmin=3$ is fixed. The objective is pooled base-pair F1 on the tuning set, with ties broken towards neutral values. We compare three tuning methods.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shared ratios, exhaustive search:} both layers share $\rinit$ and $\rext$ and $\rcross=1$; the full grid of the remaining four settings ($17\times17\times9\times9$ candidates) is scored, so the optimum is global.
    \item \textbf{Separate ratios, coordinate ascent:} all seven settings, one at a time from neutral.
    \item \textbf{Separate ratios, genetic algorithm:} all seven settings; population 32, tournament selection, uniform crossover, mutations of one or two grid steps and elitism, followed by coordinate ascent.
\end{itemize}
Base-pair counts are cached for every candidate and alignment and computed in parallel; the protocol below scores 31{,}830 candidates in 17 minutes on a laptop.

\subsection{Selecting the method}
The tuning set is split into 10 folds by clan. Each method is tuned on nine folds and predicts the tenth, and the pooled held-out predictions give its cross-validated F1 (Table~\ref{tab:cv}). The method with the highest estimate is chosen, with ties going to fewer settings, and is then fitted on the whole tuning set.

The final settings (Table~\ref{tab:settings}) keep $\rinit<\rext$ and charge each pseudoknotted pair a ratio of 0.56.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{What the second layer buys.} A nested SCFG cannot represent pseudoknots, and adding a second layer with a cost per pseudoknotted pair recovers half of the pseudoknotted pairs at a PPV of 0.60, without lowering overall accuracy. IPknot, which uses thermodynamic pair probabilities and per-level thresholds, rarely predicts pseudoknots on these alignments of 4--12 sequences.

\textbf{Where it falls short.} On alignments without pseudoknots, RNAalifold's energy model is more accurate than the phylogenetic SCFG (0.785 vs 0.755), and \GB{} still predicts a pseudoknot for 6\% of them. The KH-99 grammar has no loop-length or stacking-energy terms, and its substitution model treats sequences as related only through the tree.

\textbf{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{References.} Rfam consensus structures, even literature-derived ones, are curated alignments rather than solved three-dimensional structures, and they may miss or misplace pseudoknots.
    \item \emph{Pseudoknot model.} The second layer is a greedy decomposition into two nested layers, not a pseudoknot grammar. It cannot represent structures that need three layers, and it can miss a pseudoknot whose helices are recognisable only together. Joint refinement of both layers did not help on this benchmark.
    \item \emph{Baselines.} Single-sequence pseudoknot predictors, such as Knotify, SPOT-RNA and E2Efold, were not evaluated, and IPknot was run with its default aligned configuration only.
    \item \emph{Statistics.} With 47 pseudoknotted test alignments, pseudoknot measures have intervals about $\pm 0.1$ wide.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Alternatives not adopted.} Earlier development on a smaller data set also examined posterior (maximum-expected-accuracy) decoding, minimum stem lengths, a separate weight for unpaired columns and a smoothed tuning objective; none gave a reliable cross-validated gain.

\section{Conclusion}
\GB{} extends a phylogenetic SCFG to pseudoknots with a second parsing layer, a pseudoknot pair ratio and an evidence weight, all tuned by a cross-validated protocol on clan-disjoint Rfam data. On 246 held-out alignments it matches RNAalifold and IPknot on overall base-pair accuracy, predicts pseudoknotted pairs with F1 0.541 against 0.113 for IPknot, and runs about eight times faster than IPknot. The remaining gap to energy-based folding on nested structures points to richer loop models as the next step.
